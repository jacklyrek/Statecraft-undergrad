# Unit 2 research notes

## Core sources

- Bruce Bueno de Mesquita and Alastair Smith, The Dictator's Handbook. Essential for the logic of winning coalitions, selectorate logic, and the distribution of benefits to the people who matter.
- Niccolò Machiavelli, The Prince. The classic manual for understanding faction, military loyalty, and the trade-off between fear and affection.
- Ibn Khaldun, The Muqaddimah. Especially valuable for the relationship between cohesion, solidarity, and political longevity.
- Michael Mann, The Sources of Social Power. Useful for understanding how coercive and infrastructural power depend on support networks as well as force.
- Brian P. Leech, “Coalitions and Ruler Survival.” Useful for the comparative logic of coalition maintenance and regime durability.

## Secondary works

- James C. Scott, Domination and the Arts of Resistance. Useful for the political economy of local accommodation and the subtle forms of noncompliance.
- Gary W. Cox, The Efficient Secret. Useful for the institutional logic of how politicians manage supporters and opponents.

## Research use

Read these sources with the ruler's-eye question in mind. The point is not to identify a universal theory of coalition politics but to learn how rulers maintain support when the same policies could either preserve power or trigger defection.
