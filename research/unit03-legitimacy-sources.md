# Unit 3 research notes

## Core sources

- Max Weber, Economy and Society. The classical source for distinguishing traditional, charismatic, and rational-legal legitimacy.
- David Beetham, The Legitimation of Power. Useful for the distinction between legality, consent, and shared belief.
- Bernard Lewis, The Muslim Discovery of Europe. Useful for how dynastic and confessional legitimacy operate in historical settings where power is embedded in religious and cultural authority.
- Jack Goldstone, Revolution and Rebellion in the Early Modern World. Useful for the link between legitimacy, crisis, and regime vulnerability.
- Hannah Arendt, On Revolution. Useful for how legitimacy and legitimacy crises are experienced in moments of political breakdown.

## Secondary works

- Pierre Bourdieu, Language and Symbolic Power. Useful for how symbolic authority works in practice.
- Charles W. Mills, The Racial Contract. Useful for showing that legitimacy is never purely universal; it often depends on partial inclusion and exclusion.

## Research use

This unit should be read as a study of the political value of belief and performance. Legitimacy is not only about what a ruler is, but about what the ruled are willing to accept as reasonable and lawful.
