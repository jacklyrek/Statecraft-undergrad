# Unit 4 research notes

## Core sources

- Charles Tilly, “War Making and State Making as Organized Crime.” Essential reading for the link between warfare, coercion, extraction, and political order.
- Michael Mann, The Sources of Social Power. Useful for distinguishing coercive power from infrastructural power and for thinking about how force is embedded in broader political systems.
- James Quinlan, The Logic of Military Organization. Useful for the internal politics of force and the way military institutions become political actors.
- Samuel Huntington, Political Order in Changing Societies. Especially important for the relationship between military institutions, political development, and regime stability.

## Secondary works

- Fearon and Laitin, “Ethnicity, Insurgency, and Civil War.” Useful for the political logic of irregular violence and state fragility.
- John Keegan, A History of Warfare. Useful for understanding how force is organized differently across historical contexts.

## Research use

This unit should be read as a study of how rulers prevent force from becoming autonomous and hostile to the regime itself. The central question is not simply whether the army is effective, but whether it is politically controllable.
